An emerging collaborative research initiative

The Political Economy of Imperial Accumulation

Investigating how imperial accumulation is constituted, reproduced and transformed through historically interconnected economic and political relations.

How is imperial accumulation constituted and reproduced through interacting economic and political mechanisms, and under what conditions can those relations be transformed?

An open proposal for collaborative theoretical, historical and empirical research. The programme is at a formative stage; participation and research priorities have not yet been agreed.

01 / The explanatory problem

One historical process. Multiple points of entry.

Research on imperialism has developed important, sometimes competing explanations of unequal exchange, colonial drain, international value relations, monopoly, labour and resource appropriation, and uneven development.

These approaches do not necessarily measure the same things, operate at the same level of abstraction or share compatible assumptions. Their disagreements are important. Yet the historical processes they seek to explain may be connected in ways obscured when each explanation is examined in isolation.

This initiative proposes to investigate those connections without presuming theoretical reconciliation. The aim is to understand how particular relations of production, exchange, appropriation and political authority constitute, condition and transform one another within the wider historical process of imperial accumulation.

02 / Lines of inquiry

Questions for collective investigation

Mechanisms and relations

How do different forms of appropriation and value transfer arise, interact and change historically? Where do explanatory frameworks converge, conflict or address different objects?

Conditions of reproduction

What political, institutional, productive and material capacities sustain these relations? How are those capacities themselves produced and reproduced?

Contestation and transformation

Under what conditions can established relations of appropriation be challenged, reorganised or displaced? How do alternative capacities emerge and endure?

03 / Research approach

Explanation before reconciliation.

The initiative is conceived as a shared research problem, not a unified theoretical school. It welcomes substantive disagreement and treats the historical relationships among explanatory objects as questions to be investigated rather than connections to be assumed.

Potential activities include theoretical debate, comparative historical inquiry, transparent model reconstruction, reproducible quantitative research, and documentation of the assumptions and limitations of methods and datasets.

Quantitative results, historical claims and theoretical propositions should remain distinguishable. Apparent overlaps between measures of appropriation should not be treated as additive without establishing what each measure captures.

04 / Existing infrastructure

Building from the Unequal Exchange Toolkit

The Unequal Exchange Toolkit is an independent, open research resource documenting approaches to unequal exchange and dependency, their evidence, assumptions and limitations.

It provides a practical starting point for a wider collaborative initiative, rather than a finished platform.

05 / Participation

An invitation to develop the questions together.

This emerging initiative welcomes discussion with researchers working on imperialism, political economy, historical development, international value relations, appropriation and political transformation.

Contributions might include theoretical critiques, historical case studies, datasets, model reconstructions, research questions or collaborative discussions.

Participation does not require GitHub expertise. See the existing contribution page for ways to engage with the Toolkit.

Inclusion of an author's work in the Toolkit does not imply their endorsement of or participation in this programme.
