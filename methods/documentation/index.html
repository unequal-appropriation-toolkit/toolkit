Documentation standards

The Unequal Appropriation Toolkit documents and compares approaches to imperial accumulation, unequal exchange and appropriation without requiring a single theory, unit of analysis or technique.

Core profile questions

  1. Research object: What is investigated and how is it defined?
  2. Theoretical explanation: What relations or processes are proposed to explain it?
  3. Research design and operationalisation: How is the question investigated?
  4. Analytical procedures: Which conceptual, historical, statistical or computational procedures are used?
  5. Evidence and sources: Which texts, archives, datasets or other materials support the analysis?
  6. Outputs and inference: What is demonstrated, estimated, illustrated or interpreted, and what does not follow directly?

Four dimensions of a methodological profile

Each profile uses the same four headings. They are dimensions for comparison, not a required chronological sequence; historical, theoretical and quantitative studies may work across them differently.

  1. 1. Theoretical explanation — brings together the research object and proposed theoretical relations (core questions 1–2).
  2. 2. Research design and operationalisation — specifies the research strategy, concepts, units, boundaries and comparisons (core questions 1 and 3).
  3. 3. Analytical procedures — identifies conceptual, archival, historical, statistical or computational methods and their source basis (core questions 4–5).
  4. 4. Evidence, findings and inference — describes what is demonstrated, interpreted, illustrated or estimated and the limits of the conclusions (core questions 5–6).

The Methods Library also provides an eight-dimension comparison table (theory, estimation, technique, unit, boundary, data, interpretation and verification) and a thirteen-item documentation checklist. These are finer-grained cross-study questions, not alternative profile schemas. Verification is recorded separately from methodological documentation and is not a requirement for a complete documentation profile.

Levels of explanation

Methodological documentation distinguishes, where relevant, between the phenomenon a study seeks to explain, the evidence and operationalisations through which that phenomenon is established or characterised, and the theoretical mechanisms proposed to account for its existence and reproduction.

These levels are analytically distinguishable but not necessarily sequential or theory-independent. Research profiles should make explicit what the evidence establishes, which assumptions underpin the analysis, and where further theoretical or causal inference begins.

Three separate status records

Documentation status describes the extent and source basis of the Toolkit's account. Author review records whether the author has checked or contributed to the profile. Research extensions identify additional reconstruction, replication or analysis undertaken by the Toolkit.

Author review is welcome but does not imply endorsement of the Toolkit's classifications or of other entries. Independent replication is optional and is not a prerequisite for documenting a study.

Provenance and access

Profiles identify the materials on which their documentation is based and distinguish published sources from privately shared or independently reconstructed materials. Privately shared manuscripts or datasets are not redistributed without permission.

Where a profile is preliminary, that refers to the state of the Toolkit's documentation, not a judgement on the underlying scholarship.

This standard is provisional and may be refined through scholarly discussion.
