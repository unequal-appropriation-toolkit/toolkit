The Law of World Value

Alan Freeman

Value, prices and monetary relations · primaryDevelopment and structural differentiation

Documentation status: Substantive methodological profile; author review pending. The manuscript and supporting data were shared privately and are not redistributed. The underlying data have not been independently examined.

Profile provenance: Toolkit-prepared from an author-shared submission manuscript; author review invited.

Methodological architecture

01 / Theoretical explanation

Investigates why international monetary divergence persists. Freeman argues that world-market prices can remain systematically separated from labour values, generating unequal realisation gains and losses. These advantages reinforce Northern technological rents and investment capacity, reproducing structural differentiation.

02 / Research design and operationalisation

Distinguishes monetary purchasing power, consumption and productive contribution. Constructs historically defined comparison blocs, treating China and the former Soviet/Eastern bloc separately, and compares output realised at world-market prices with productive contributions approximated through employment-based labour time.

03 / Analytical procedures

Uses the Monetary Inequality Index (MII), between- and within-bloc Theil decomposition, an Employee Inequality Index (EII), comparisons of shares of value produced and realised, and a world monetary expression of labour time (MELT). Data include Penn national accounts and World Economic Outlook series; employment-based calculations require further documentation.

04 / Evidence, findings and inference

Presents long-run monetary divergence, bloc comparisons, and estimates of unequal realisation relative to labour-based productive contributions. The MII establishes a monetary pattern, not a causal mechanism by itself; the labour-value comparisons support a distinct claim about unequal realisation, while the account of its reproduction remains a theoretical explanation to investigate.

Research profile

Investigates persistent international monetary divergence and proposes a pluralist research programme. The manuscript moves from documenting monetary inequality to comparing labour-based value produced with monetary value realised, and advances an explanation of cumulative world-market advantages.

Research object
International inequality between historically differentiated country blocs, especially the divergence between productive contribution and monetary realisation in the world market.
Theoretical explanation
Persistent world-market price–value differences create unequal realisation gains and losses. Freeman proposes a cumulative process in which Northern profits, technological rents and investment in upstream capabilities sustain advantages, while Southern economies face constraints on building comparable productive capacities. He distinguishes this from the narrower measurement of value crossing borders in trade.
Research design
Historically defines North and South comparators, with China and the former Soviet/Eastern bloc considered separately. Distinguishes monetary inequality (market-exchange-rate GDP), consumption (PPP), and productive contribution (labour time, initially approximated by employment). Examines sensitivity to country grouping and historical period.
Analytical procedures
Monetary Inequality Index (MII); Theil decomposition of interbloc and intrabloc inequality; Employee Inequality Index (EII); comparison of shares of world value produced and realised; and a world monetary expression of labour time (MELT). National accounts, World Economic Outlook data and employment measures underpin different components.
Outputs and limits of inference
Reports long-run monetary divergence, labour-based estimates of productive contribution, monetary realisation and their differences. The MII alone does not quantify value transfers or demonstrate causation. Employment is an imperfect labour-time proxy; hours, informal work, finance, embodied past labour, missing observations and bloc boundaries require careful treatment. The causal account of cumulative technological advantage is a theoretical interpretation, not something established by the MII alone.
