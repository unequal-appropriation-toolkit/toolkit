Follow the flows.
Question the relations.

What is being appropriated? What makes that appropriation unequal? And how do the concepts and methods we use shape what we can identify, measure and explain?

The Unequal Appropriation Toolkit is an open research resource for investigating these questions across different approaches to unequal exchange, economic dependency and the organisation of the world economy.

Research on value transfers, labour, resources and surplus does not necessarily identify the same processes or measure interchangeable quantities. The Toolkit documents how different approaches define their objects, establish inequality, select evidence and construct their findings. It makes these distinctions available for scrutiny and further investigation without presuming theoretical agreement.

01 / The toolkit

Different tools.
Your questions.

Explore theoretical approaches, historical research, empirical methods and experimental tools. Each offers a particular way of asking questions about economic relations and the possibilities for changing them.

The Toolkit makes the assumptions, evidence, procedures and limits of these approaches easier to examine. They can inform one another without their findings being treated as directly comparable or their differences being resolved in advance.

The guiding questions

What is appropriated?
What makes it unequal?

A flow of labour, resources or value is not, by itself, an explanation of appropriation. Different approaches identify different relations, mechanisms and standards against which inequality is assessed.

The Toolkit asks how those distinctions affect what researchers can establish about economic dependency, political and economic power, and the reproduction of unequal relations. It also provides space to investigate how those relations might be reorganised, and what capacities such changes could make possible.

02 / Open development

Built openly.
Developed carefully.

The Toolkit is an evolving research initiative. Methods, datasets and experimental instruments are documented alongside their assumptions, limitations and verification status.

Research materials are not presented as independently validated simply because they are available. Unresolved questions, partial reconstructions and planned work are recorded explicitly.
