Unequal appropriation is not simply a question of who gets what. It is a question of the relations through which labour, resources, value and wealth are produced, transferred and appropriated — and what makes those relations unequal.

The Unequal Appropriation Toolkit is an open research resource for investigating unequal exchange, economic dependency and the organisation of the world economy.

Different theories identify different processes, propose different explanations of inequality and produce different kinds of evidence. The Toolkit makes their concepts, methods and findings available for comparison and scrutiny — without assuming they describe the same phenomena or must reach the same conclusions.

01 / The conceptual question

Not every unequal flow
explains an unequal relation.

A measured difference in flows, prices or incomes does not, on its own, tell us whether appropriation has occurred, why a relation is unequal, or how that inequality is reproduced.

01

What is appropriated?

Labour, resources, value, surplus and financial claims are not interchangeable research objects. Different approaches define and trace them differently.

02

What makes it unequal?

Asymmetry must be interpreted. Which relations, mechanisms and standards make a difference evidence of unequal appropriation?

03

What can the evidence establish?

Methods illuminate some processes while leaving others unresolved. The Toolkit distinguishes what is measured, what is inferred and what remains contested.

“Unequal appropriation” is an organising question, not a single theory. It creates space to compare approaches without collapsing their objects, measures or explanations into one another.

03 / Open development

Built openly.
Developed carefully.

The Toolkit is an evolving research initiative. Methods, datasets and experimental instruments are documented alongside their assumptions, limitations and verification status.

Research materials are not presented as independently validated simply because they are available. Unresolved questions, partial reconstructions and planned work are recorded explicitly.
